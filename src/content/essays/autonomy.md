# The Autonomy Manifesto: A New Settlement for the AI Era

## The Broken Promise

Something is wrong and almost everyone knows it.

If you lean left, you see a rigged economy: rents that devour wages, monopolies that crush fair exchange, a planet burning while incumbents extract. You see working people squeezed between stagnant pay and rising costs, their autonomy a fiction.

If you lean right, you see a bloated state: taxes on honest work, regulations that suffocate enterprise, a bureaucracy that treats citizens as dependants rather than adults. You see freedom eroded one intervention at a time.

You are both correct. And you are both being played.

The truth is that the current system fails on its own terms. It taxes the wrong things, protects the wrong people, and subsidises the wrong behaviour. It punishes the baker for selling bread while rewarding the landlord for owning the street. It lets corporations dump their costs on the public and then asks the public to fund the cleanup from their pay cheques. It has constructed an economy of gatekeepers, middlemen, and rent-seekers who produce nothing and capture everything — and then calls this arrangement "the free market."

It is not a free market. It is theft dressed in ideology.

## The Reckoning That Is Coming

This broken settlement is about to collide with the most disruptive force in economic history. Within a few years, artificial intelligence will render most white-collar work economically unviable. Not some of it. Most of it. The cognitive labour that sustains the middle class — the reports, the analyses, the administration, the coordination — will be done better, faster, and almost for free by machines.

Under the current model, this is a catastrophe. Millions stripped of income, purpose, and dignity, while the owners of capital accumulate wealth beyond comprehension. The social contract — trade your labour for survival — will snap, and nothing in the existing political toolkit can repair it. Not retraining. Not regulation. Not nostalgia.

But this is only a catastrophe if we cling to a settlement that was already failing. Seen clearly, the AI revolution is not a crisis. It is an invitation — perhaps the last one we will receive — to build something that actually works.

## The Principle

Every political philosophy rests on a foundation. Ours is **autonomy**: the freedom of every individual to act, to choose, to build a life on their own terms — bounded only by the oldest moral law there is. Do not harm others.

This is not a left-wing idea. It is not a right-wing idea. It is the only idea consistent with treating human beings as adults. Everything that follows is a deduction from this single principle, not a wish list, not a compromise — a logical consequence.

## What Follows

**If autonomy is paramount, then fair participation in the economy is a right, not a privilege.** A fair price is the market price in an open, efficient market — after those who impose costs on others have compensated for them. Carbon taxes, congestion fees, pollution levies: these are not punishments. They are the golden rule applied to economics. You pay for what you damage.

**If fair value requires open markets, then monopoly is a violation of the founding principle.** The primary role of government narrows to two functions: ensure markets remain open and efficient, and enforce the payment of externality costs. Everything else — the regulations, the subsidies, the preferential treatment — is the architecture of capture, and it must go.

**If scarce resources belong to everyone, then their value must be returned to everyone.** Land, spectrum, the atmosphere's capacity to absorb emissions — these are not products of anyone's labour. Their market value belongs to the public. A land value tax and environmental levies become the primary source of state revenue, and every citizen receives their share as a dividend. Not charity. Not welfare. A rightful return on common ownership.

**If the state does not tax your labour, trade, or enterprise, then it has no business engineering your employment.** No means-tested benefits. No minimum wage. At-will employment. This is not cruelty — it is the consequence of having already guaranteed the floor. When basics are abundant and every citizen holds a dividend, the scaffolding of worker protection solves a problem that no longer exists.

**If individual liberty is paramount, then speech, thought, and belief are inviolable.** The only restraint society may impose on liberty is the prevention of demonstrable harm to others. Being offended is not harm — and to enshrine offence as a ground for censorship is to hand every faction a veto over every other.

**If justice exists to correct unfairness, then it must distinguish unfairness from misfortune.** Unlucky is not unjust. But deliberate harm — fraud, coercion, the manipulation of another into an unfair price — demands remedy. Justice is the enforcement of the golden rule, not the redistribution of luck.

**If nations may make their own tradeoffs, they may not export the costs.** A country that burns more carbon to gain a trade advantage is dumping its externalities on the world. Border adjustments are not protectionism. They are the golden rule applied between nations.

## The Liberation

Now return to the AI revolution and see it differently.

In this model, the displacement of white-collar work is not a disaster. It is a liberation. When the floor is guaranteed — when housing, food, energy, and transport are abundant and cheap, when every citizen draws a dividend from the wealth of the commons — then the question is no longer *how do I survive?* It is *what do I want to do?*

Start a hobby business. Master a craft. Coach a youth team. Write the book. Build the community garden. Run the local club. These are not consolation prizes for the economically displaced. They are better expressions of human value than forty years in a cubicle producing work a machine now does in seconds.

The old economy measured human worth in wages. The new one can measure it in meaning — but only if we are brave enough to change the rules before the old ones break.

## The Choice

The status quo is not stable. It is a system optimised for a world that no longer exists, propped up by inertia and defended by those who profit from its distortions. The left and the right argue endlessly about how to administer a broken machine. We propose to replace it.

This is not utopia. It is arithmetic. Tax what is taken from the commons. Stop taxing what people create. Let fair value, not incumbency, determine who thrives. Trust adults with freedom and hold them to one rule: do no harm.

The technology is arriving whether we are ready or not. The only question is whether we meet it with a system designed for human autonomy — or one that was already failing before the machines showed up.

Choose.
